ML Vol Forecasting

Timing the Variance Seller

A machine-learned realized-variance forecast as a daily trade / stand-aside signal for the GSVIVS01 index

Ryan · July 2026

The product and its problem

GSVIVS01 sells variance every single day

Each morning it sells a strip of same-day SPX options that replicates a variance swap, delta-hedges through the day, and settles at the close. Index level 100 to 138 in four years, roughly 9.6 points a year.

The gains are steady. The losses arrive on the few days when realized variance exceeds the strike it sold, and the index has no opinion about when those days come.

The claim

Every morning: compare the forecast to the strike

At 09:10, before the strip is sold, the model's overnight forecast of today's realized variance is compared with the strike on offer. Variance rich: sell as usual. Forecast above the strike: stand aside for the day.

the same OTM-option integral as the VIX, which is why the strike sits above ATM implied vol: it inherits the skew

Annualized Sharpe 1.60 → 1.95, backtest May 2022 to Jun 2026

The model

A linear spine and a tree overlay

The spine is HAR-IV: a four-parameter regression on today's, last week's and last month's realized variance, plus implied vol. It alone carries most of the forecast.

LightGBM starts from the spine's prediction and learns only what is left over, trained end to end on the same loss we judge it by.

Each horizon reads the option tenor that expires with it: the 1-day forecast uses same-day IV, the 5-day uses 1-week, the 22-day uses 1-month.

The features

Four things the market tells you

About 128 inputs once every series also contributes its daily change and how unusual it is against its own recent history.

Why trust the number

Walk-forward with a moat, five seeds

Training always ends ten trading days before testing begins, on every fold, because the target itself overlaps days. Splits are by date across all 21 symbols, so no symbol leaks the future to another. Even the early-stopping check sits behind its own gap.

proportional error, so calm markets count as much as crises, and underprediction hurts more, as it should for an option seller. One lucky seed looked 6% better than the truth; every headline number is a five-seed mean.

What it learned

Everything it learned has a name you know

SHAP splits every individual forecast into named feature contributions that sum exactly to the prediction, so we can audit what the trees add on top of the linear spine.

What tops the list: the implied-to-realized relationship changing with regime, extremes of the variance risk premium, Fed-meeting proximity, and unusually-high-against-own-history flags.

Results

Where it wins, and where it honestly doesn't

1-day ahead: about 10% lower forecast loss than the strongest linear baseline, statistically significant. 5-day: about 11% lower, significant. 22-day: the four-parameter linear model wins; at a monthly horizon the option market has already done the work.

And the loss function is the product: the identical model trained on MSE instead of QLIKE trades at Sharpe 0.3.

The fine print, and the point

Three caveats, three numbers

The backtest strike is a proxy from the index's own marks; it tracks the real strike almost perfectly (correlation above 0.99) and the production feed exists. The edge is concentrated in about ten signal changes a year, so each call matters. COVID only enters training from 2022 onward, by construction.

# Deep Research: Most-realistic, cleanly-evaluable IV-RV-gap delta-hedged straddle backtest

> The strategy: short the delta-hedged straddle when lagged implied vol exceeds my model's RV forecast, long when below. Goal = the most realistic version that still yields clean, testable metrics (pooled and deflated Sharpe, plus a link back to the QLIKE accuracy of the RV forecast). For each of the 7 components: where my choice is standard, where it is flawed/outdated, what I'm missing, with named closed-form results and "where this breaks" caveats.

**Date:** 2026-06-01-realistic-ivrv-straddle-backtest · **Sources:** 24 kept of 68 harvested · **Lens:** state of the art, 2025-2026 · **Universe priority:** equity-index + liquid single-name options, intraday (5-min) data

## Direct Answer

The current best practice for this exact pipeline is now codified by **Pollok (2025, arXiv:2506.07928)** — daily ATM delta-neutral straddles on S&P 500 single names sorted on a lagged VRP gap `f(RV_hat_t, IV_{t-1})`, evaluated by **both** statistical loss (MSE/MAE/QLIKE/Mincer-Zarnowitz R²) **and** realized straddle portfolio returns, with the lookahead-safe protocol of measuring the predictor at 3:55pm ET, trading before the 4pm close, and realizing returns the next day. Your component-level choices are mostly standard and defensible, with five concrete upgrades: (1) the PnL identity `0.5·Γ·S²·(RV − IV²/252)` is the Ahmad-Wilmott / Carr-Madan **continuous-hedging-at-implied** dollar-gamma result, path-dependent and valid only for a near-ATM diffusion with no jumps; (2) **do not attribute the 1/N-and-kurtosis hedging-error variance to Ahmad-Wilmott** — their closed form is a *stock-path* variance with **no 1/N and no kurtosis term**; the discrete-rebalancing variance you want is **Boyle-Emanuel (1980)**, and the κ-inflation is your own leptokurtic extension; (3) charge the **option** spread event-driven (entry/flip/exit) but run it as a *sensitivity band* — full quoted half-spread is the make-or-break pessimistic case (Goyal-Saretto straddle alpha collapses 22.7%→3.9%/month at quoted spread, Muravyev-Pearson) and a timing-aware effective spread of ~⅓–½ of that is the realistic case; (4) treat Leland (1985) as a **deterministic cost-line inflator, not the optimal policy** — it is now a baseline that NN/band hedgers beat, and under *constant* costs its error does not vanish (Kabanov-Safarian 1997); (5) deflated Sharpe (Bailey-Lopez de Prado 2014) is the right gate but the denominator uses the **observed** SR̂ (not SR₀) and **non-annualized** inputs. **Biggest caveat:** every economic-value source here is daily-frequency, index-or-single-name, and 1993-2023 — none validates the *intraday 5-min* hedging-error and cost mechanics you are building, so facets 4-6 must be confirmed by your own bar-by-bar simulation, not borrowed.

## Key Insights

- **Signal / lookahead protocol (facet 1).** The cleanest stated anti-lookahead convention: train/update the RV model on info between the t−1 close and *before 3:55pm ET on t*; measure the predictor `x_t ∈ F_t^{3:55}`; execute the straddle before the 4pm close on t; realize returns on t+1. De-annualize IV by `/√250` to put it in daily RV units before differencing. Signal `X_{t-1}=VRP_{t-1}=f(RV_hat_t, IV_{t-1})` with `f ∈ {x−y, x/y, ln(x/y)}` (Pollok 2025, §3.2-3.3 Eq.17 + abstract, arxiv.org/abs/2506.07928) [preprint]. Your lagged-IV-vs-RV-forecast design is exactly this.
- **Sizing beats binary threshold (facet 1).** Graded/confidence-scaled sizing delivers the highest Sharpe; both delta-neutral (no scaling) and maximally-aggressive scaling are dominated by *moderate* scaling (Li & Wu 2026, FRL 87:109098, abstract; DOI 10.1016/j.frl.2025.109098) [peer-reviewed]. **Caveat:** this is a *directional* ML delta-scaling result on a single ETF, **not** a VRP/straddle/RV study — cite only for "graded sizing > binary," nothing else.
- **PnL identity is the hedge-at-implied dollar-gamma result (facet 2).** Daily MTM `= ½(σ²−σ̃²)·S²·Γⁱ·dt`, deterministic per day (no dX term); total PV `= ½(σ²−σ̃²)·∫e^{−r(t−t₀)}S²Γⁱ dt`, "always positive but highly path dependent" (Ahmad-Wilmott 2005, §4 Eq.1, pp.67; full PDF spekulant.com.pl) [practitioner]. Built on the Carr(2005)/Henrard(2003) `(σ²−σ_h²)` dollar-gamma identity (their Eq.2, p.67). Your `0.5·Γ·S²·(RV − IV²/252)` is the discretized daily version with σ̃²→IV²/252.
- **A single straddle is NOT a clean variance bet (facet 2).** The gamma weight `S_t²Γ_t` peaks ATM and collapses as spot drifts off-strike, so the straddle realized PnL is path-dependent and vega/vanna/volga take over off-strike; the variance *swap* removes this via the log contract = strip of OTM options weighted `1/K²` + dynamic `1/S` (Carr-Madan 2002; grounded via Carr-Lee 2009 Annu.Rev.Fin.Econ. Eqs.9-10 pp.323-328) [peer-reviewed]. **Where it breaks:** requires continuous paths, no jumps; the leading discretization/jump error is *third order in the daily return* (Carr-Lee Eq.9), not a clean second-order gamma residual.
- **Discrete-hedging error: 1/√N std, mean-absolute O(N^{-1/2}) (facets 2,5).** RMSE of the tracking error `= g/√N + O(1/N)`, `√N·error ⇒` a mixed-normal (conditionally-Gaussian) limit driven by integrated squared dollar-gamma; granularity `g` per Eq.2.18 (Bertsimas-Kogan-Lo 2000, Thm 1c Eq.2.13 p.10-11, Eq.2.18 p.13, MIT PDF) [peer-reviewed]. **Vanilla (piecewise-linear) straddle payoffs are governed by their Theorem 2 (Eq.2.17 p.12), not Theorem 1** (which needs a 6×-differentiable payoff). This is the diffusion-case backbone for deterministic 1/N Sharpe-denominator inflation.
- **The kurtosis/1-over-N variance is Boyle-Emanuel, NOT Ahmad-Wilmott (facet 5 — correction).** Ahmad-Wilmott's closed-form variance (Result 2 = `G(S0,t0)−F(S0,t0)²`, §4.2 Eq.10, p.70) is a *continuous-hedging stock-path* variance with **zero** `1/N` and **zero** kurtosis terms — the words "kurtosis", "Boyle", "Emanuel" appear 0 times (Ahmad-Wilmott 2005, verified full-text) [practitioner]. The per-rebalance error `H_i = ½ΓS²σ²dt(x_i²−1)`, `x_i²~χ²₁` (skewed, leptokurtic per step; Gaussian only after aggregation), total std `∝ ½S²σ²(T−t)Γ√(1/N)` is **Boyle-Emanuel (1980, JFE 8(3):259-282)**, reproduced in Anagnou-Hodges (2007, Eq.7 p.9, χ² statement pp.12-13, EFMA PDF) [the reproduction is non-refereed; the underlying result is peer-reviewed].
- **The κ-inflation (`∝ σ⁴·(κ−1)/N`) is YOUR extension — re-derive it, don't cite it (facet 5).** Substituting `Var(x_i²)=κ−1` (κ=3 Gaussian gives 2) into Boyle-Emanuel is **not in Boyle-Emanuel, not in Anagnou-Hodges, and not in Ahmad-Wilmott** (verified: Anagnou-Hodges keeps `var(u²)=2`). Bennett's vega-form packaging `σ_PnL = Vega·σ·√(π/4N)` follows the half-normal E|·| convention; note the coefficient tension (√(π/4)≈0.886 half-normal vs 1/√2≈0.707 L2-std form) depends on whether you report E|·| or L2 std (Freeport note relaying Bennett, Trading Volatility p.95) [practitioner, context-only].
- **Where the analytic 1/N diverges from a bar-by-bar sim (facet 5).** For pure-jump Lévy, `lim n·E[ε_T²] > 0` and *may be infinite* — variance does NOT vanish at the 1/N rate (Broden-Tankov 2010, arXiv:1003.0709) [preprint]; for vanilla calls/puts the 1/N rate survives but with a constant inflated by the jump component; for digital/barrier payoffs the rate is strictly slower than 1/√N. **Implication: deterministic 1/N inflation is a LOWER BOUND once you admit overnight gaps / jumps / vol clustering — your intraday sim will show a non-vanishing variance floor the formula misses.**
- **Option costs are the make-or-break assumption (facet 3).** Quoted spread on the most liquid US equity options averages 8.1¢/share; conventional effective 6.2¢ on a $1.70 avg option (≈4-5% round-trip of premium); timing-aware effective is only **1.3¢ = 21% of conventional, 16% of quoted** (Muravyev-Pearson 2015/RFS 2020, intro pp.1-3, cicfconf PDF) [peer-reviewed]. Strategy-level: Goyal-Saretto (2009) long-short straddle decile collapses **22.7%→3.9%/month** at the quoted spread; Driessen-Maenhout-Vilkov alpha goes insignificant (same paper, p.6). **Run the backtest under BOTH a quoted-half-spread (pessimistic) and timing-adjusted ~⅓-½ (realistic) cost band.** Cent *levels* are 2003-06 vintage; the *lesson* (timing-aware ≈ ⅓-⅕ of quoted) is durable.
- **Realistic maturity-resolved cost schedule (facet 3).** For liquid 21-48 DTE ATM SPX the mean *effective* spread is **~2% of premium** (≈4% if you charge the full quoted spread), rising to ~3.5% at 7-13 DTE and ~9% at 0DTE; "up to 10% of price" is the 0DTE/stressed upper bound only (Doshi-Pari-Shamsuddin 2025, Table 2 p.46 + p.19, bauer.uh.edu PDF) [preprint]. Spreads spike on the 3rd-Friday monthly expiry (roll dates) — defensible to up-weight roll-date cost, but that's a modeling choice, not a measured multiplier. **Cross-venue routing savings apply to single names (16 exchanges, CBOE ~20%) but NOT to CBOE-exclusive SPX.**
- **Event-driven half-spread costing is standard current practice (facet 3).** A 2024 SPX 1-min CBOE backtest fills at midpoint + half the bid-ask on every execution (entry, exit, each hedge), applied to *both* the option and the ETF delta-hedge leg, no daily amortization; hedge frequency (30/130/1440 min) is treated as a tunable trading off replication error vs accumulated cost (Wysocki-Slepaczuk 2024, §2.3 + Table 1, arXiv:2407.13908) [preprint; the "event-driven vs amortized" framing is inference, not stated]. The most on-point frontier source charges option costs as a **percentage on every position change** `κ₂ ∈ {0.5,1,1.5,2}%` vs underlying `κ₁=0.05%` — the vega/vol-point equivalent of what you're converging on (François et al. 2025, §4.1 Eq.1, arXiv:2504.06208) [preprint].
- **Leland (1985) is a heuristic cost-line inflator, not the optimal policy (facet 4).** Augmented vol `σ̂² = σ²(1 + √(2/π)·k/(σ√dt))`, where `√(2/π)≈0.798` is the half-normal mean of `|ΔH|` and `k` = round-trip proportional cost, `dt` = revision interval (Zhao-Ziemba 2003, Eq.5 p.8, d-nb.info PDF) [preprint, 2003 baseline]. **Where it breaks:** under *constant* (n-independent) costs the Leland portfolio does NOT converge to the payoff — limiting hedging error is nonzero and negative, i.e. systematic under-hedging (Kabanov-Safarian 1997, FS 1:239-250) [peer-reviewed]. The rate that *rescues* it: with `k_n = k₀·n^{-1/2}` the **MSE converges at n^{-1} (RMSE at n^{-1/2})** for convex payoffs — a straddle is convex (Lepinette-Kabanov 2010, FS 14(4):625-667, Thm 1.2 Eq.1.11 / Thm 1.3 Eq.1.16) [peer-reviewed]. Modern frontier characterizes Leland as a heuristic bump that "does not arise from utility maximisation and does not characterise the optimal trading policy" and uses the Whalley-Wilmott band `h_WW=(3λδS Γ²/(2γ))^{1/3}` instead (Arzel-Lehdili 2026, arXiv:2603.29994) [preprint]; NN hedgers beat Leland under 0.5% costs (Brugière-Turinici 2025, codes `ν²=σ²+α/√dt`, "costs do not apply at inception or maturity", arXiv:2505.22836) [preprint].
- **Kurtosis calibration κ=4.0 is defensible but a simplification (facet 6).** Realized kurtosis from 5-min quartic returns scaled by RV² is statistically reliable and robust to microstructure noise (Monte Carlo, jump-robust RV, subsampling verified) — RV is the noise-sensitive moment, not RM4 (Amaya-Christoffersen-Jacobs-Vasquez 2015, JFE 118(1):135-167, robustness §2-3) [peer-reviewed]. **But single-name realized kurtosis is systematically HIGH for small-caps, high-B/M, low-beta names** — κ is cross-sectionally heterogeneous, so one conservative κ=4.0 across a single-name universe is a real simplification (same paper, cross-section §4-5). Realized higher moments are **interval-variant**: they do NOT converge to sample skew/kurtosis and depend on the holding+sampling interval, so your 5→15-min CLT aggregation is a *stated convention, not a free lunch* (Ahadzie-Jeyasreedharan 2020, QF 20(7):1169-1184, abstract) [peer-reviewed].
- **Evaluation backbone (facet 7).** Deflated Sharpe `DSR = Z[(SR̂−SR₀)·√(T−1) / √(1 − γ₃·SR̂ + ((γ₄−1)/4)·SR̂²)]` with threshold `SR₀ = √V[SR_n]·[(1−γ)Z⁻¹(1−1/N) + γ·Z⁻¹(1−1/(Ne))]`, γ≈0.5772 Euler-Mascheroni (Bailey-Lopez de Prado 2014, JPM 40(5), Eq.2 p.8 + Eq.1 p.7) [peer-reviewed]. **Two implementation traps:** the denominator uses **observed SR̂, not SR₀** (the Wikipedia snippet that puts SR₀ there is wrong — confirmed against the paper's p.10 worked example); and inputs are **non-annualized** at native frequency with T = number of observations. The QLIKE→economic-value bridge: "marginal improvements to standard forecast error measurements can lead to economically significant gains in portfolio performance" while "it is hard to significantly and unambiguously beat the benchmark models" on QLIKE alone — i.e. economic evaluation separates models that statistical loss cannot (Pollok 2025, abstract + intro, arXiv:2506.07928) [preprint]. **This is the single best cite for facet 7's statistical-vs-economic link — but for the qualitative bridge, NOT for numbers (Results §6 not rendered/verified).**

## Contradictions & Open Threads

- **1/N analytic variance vs jump/gap reality (facets 5-6).** Boyle-Emanuel/BKL give a clean `∝1/N` (diffusion); Broden-Tankov show under jumps the limit is positive and possibly infinite, so 1/N *understates*. **Experiment:** on your 5-min data, run a full path-dependent bar-by-bar hedging-error simulation for a near-ATM index straddle at N∈{1,2,...,26} intraday rebalances; fit `Var(error) = a/N + b`. If `b` (the floor) is materially > 0 and the deterministic `σ⁴(κ−1)/N` line lies below the sim at high N, **report the sim variance, not the analytic one**, in the Sharpe denominator. Resolve κ first: estimate per-symbol 5-min realized kurtosis (Amaya et al. estimator) rather than imposing κ=4.0, and test sensitivity of the deflated Sharpe to κ∈{3,4,6}.
- **Quoted vs timing-aware option cost (facet 3).** Muravyev-Pearson: realized cost can be ⅓-⅕ of quoted for intelligent execution; the strategy's sign of profitability flips between the two (Goyal-Saretto 22.7%→3.9%). **Experiment:** compute pooled Sharpe under (a) full quoted half-spread, (b) Doshi maturity-resolved effective (~2% at 21-48 DTE), (c) timing-aware ~⅓-quoted; report the band. The strategy is only credible if it survives (a) or at minimum (b). Up-weight roll-date spreads as a robustness check.
- **Statistical (QLIKE/DM/MCS) vs economic separation (facet 7).** Pollok: QLIKE rankings are "ambiguous" yet straddle PnL separates models — but his statistical toolkit is MSE/MAE/QLIKE/MZ-R² only, **NOT DM or MCS** (those don't appear in the rendered text). **Open thread:** there is still NO formally-cited theorem mapping a *DM-significant or MCS-surviving* QLIKE gain to a guaranteed straddle-PnL improvement. **Experiment:** on your data, regress per-(symbol,day) straddle PnL on the realized `(RV − IV²/252)` and on your forecast error `(RV − RV_hat)`; test whether the QLIKE-better model's residual edge is DM-significant *and* whether that DM stat predicts cross-sectional Sharpe. This is the bridge the literature gestures at but does not prove.
- **Pooled (symbol,day) vs averaged-per-symbol Sharpe (facet 7).** No source in the dossier adjudicates this directly. Pooling inflates N and ignores cross-sectional dependence (clustered errors on common-vol days); per-symbol averaging discards the cross-section. **Experiment:** compute both; for pooled, use block-bootstrap by *day* (not by observation) to respect the cross-sectional correlation, and report the deflated Sharpe with N = number of model variations actually tried (the design's SR₀=√(2ln20)=2.45 assumes N=20 — make N honest).

# Deep Research: Forecasting SPX Realized Volatility with Cross-Asset GNNs and Regime Detection

**Date:** 2026-07-06 · **Slug:** spx-rv-gnn-regime-pipeline · **Sources:** 15 kept of 58 harvested · **Lens:** state of the art, 2025-2026

> Master objective (abridged): design a production-grade SPX RV forecaster with (1) a cross-asset GNN spillover layer, (2) a market-regime detector, (3) an integration layer fusing both. Part I (GNN) was swept same-day in `notes/deep-research/2026-07-06-gnn-cross-asset-vol.md` — this sweep targets the repo's documented near-zero gap: **regime detection (Part II) and regime×graph fusion (Part III)**.

## Direct Answer

The state of the art for adding a regime layer to an RV forecaster is **filtered (real-time) regime probabilities from a Markov-switching model injected as an extra feature into a HAR-family core** — the cleanest 2026 exemplar improves HARQ QLIKE by ~5.1% (0.171013 → 0.162259) with DM significance in 3 of 4 conditional splits (Fang & Ślepaczuk 2026, arXiv 2606.09478, Tables 2/4), and the strongest peer-reviewed evidence says Markov-switching HAR with time-varying transition probabilities beats plain HAR out-of-sample at weekly/monthly horizons but is *mixed at the daily horizon* (Ding, Kambouroudis & McMillan 2025, IREF 101, Tables 4-7, panel includes SPX). For the detector itself, the frontier has moved past vanilla HMMs to **statistical jump models** (k-means loss + jump penalty), which cut regime flickering from 9.7 to 0.4-2.7 shifts/yr and beat HMMs on point-in-time SPX signals (Shu, Yu & Mulvey 2024, J. Asset Mgmt, Tables 3-4). The regime×GNN fusion layer (Part III) is **genuinely open territory**: the only two published implementations are a "regime-dependent GNN" with no actual regime mechanism (Kumar et al. 2026, MDPI Mathematics 14(2):289) and a Hurst-triggered graph-swap that loses to naive periodic retraining in the 2008/2020 crashes (Cho & Lee 2025, Fractal Fract 9(6):339). Confidence: moderate-high that a filtered-probability feature is the right first build; the single biggest caveat is that **no quantified regime win in this dossier is on SPX intraday RV under QLIKE** — the strongest numbers come from CSI 300, EUR/USD, Latin American indices, and country-ETF panels, and the counter-evidence (MS-GARCH QLIKE ratios > 1 vs plain GARCH; Fed-FEDS THAR edge dying under QLIKE) shows the regime edge is loss-function- and benchmark-fragile.

## Key Insights

- **Filtered regime probability as a HARQ feature is the best-documented injection pattern.** Two-state MS-GJR-GARCH (Student-t) fitted to HARQ residuals, filtered probability p_t re-injected as a HARQ regressor: QLIKE 0.171013 → 0.162259 (-5.1%), MSE(log) 0.279911 → 0.267345, MZ R² 0.5215 → 0.5398 (Fang & Ślepaczuk 2026, arXiv 2606.09478, Tables 2-3, §5.1.2-5.1.3; CSI 300 5-min RV, 2,326 OOS obs). Explicit leakage discipline: filtered probabilities used "because they depend only on contemporaneously available information" (§4.1.4). [preprint, CSI 300 not SPX, unreplicated]
- **The "regimes only help in turbulence" story is NOT supported by the same paper's own tables.** Table 4 (§5.1.4) has four conditional DM tests: median-split high-vol DM=2.58 (p=0.0049), median-split low-vol DM=1.39 (p=0.0824, n.s.), top-25% high-vol DM=2.06 (p=0.0195), and — strongest of all — top-25% *low/normal-vol* DM=3.09 (p=0.0010, n=1744). Gains are broad-based, not crisis-only (Fang & Ślepaczuk 2026, Table 4). This directly informs the repo's open thread since 2026-05-12. [preprint]
- **Peer-reviewed MS-HAR verdict: horizons matter.** On 8 international indices *including SPX* (2007-2021), regime-switching HAR beats linear HAR OOS, but the edge is "less clear-cut at the daily horizon" — MS-TVTP-HAR (time-varying transition probabilities) is best across all indices at one-week-ahead; abrupt Markov-switching beats smooth-transition specifications; MCS at 90% (Ding, Kambouroudis & McMillan 2025, IREF vol. 101, DOI 10.1016/j.iref.2025.104171, Tables 4-7; open full text at storre.stir.ac.uk/bitstream/1893/37126/1/). Caveat: the paper never states whether its recursive OOS forecasts use filtered or smoothed probabilities — unresolved, not assumed safe. [peer-reviewed]
- **Regime layers can actively hurt point-vol forecasts.** MS-GARCH(1,1) and GJR-GARCH(1,1) both yield MSFE and QLIKE loss ratios > 1 vs a plain expanding-window GARCH(1,1) across four Latin American indices, 2000-2024 (JRFM 18(9):494, 2025, Table 6 + abstract; authors per PDF filename Chung/Espinoza/Quispe, unverified due to bot-block). Replicates the repo's Fed-FEDS pattern (regime edge dies under QLIKE). Caveats: benchmark is GARCH not HAR; vol proxy is cumulative squared daily returns, not intraday RV. [peer-reviewed, MDPI-tier]
- **MS-GARCH's established wins are tail-risk, not point volatility.** The definitive large-scale study finds MS-GARCH beats single-regime GARCH on VaR/ES/left-tail density for equities, with Student-t innovations doing much of the work (10-25% VaR/ES error reductions vs Gaussian) — QLIKE point-vol wins are not the headline (Ardia, Bluteau, Boudt & Catania 2018, IJF 34(4):733-747; PDF held at `reference/project-papers/b-ml-rv-core/`; claims from harvest, not adversarially verified this run). Companion R package: keblu/MSGARCH. [peer-reviewed]
- **Statistical jump models are the current answer to HMM flickering.** Jump penalty cuts regime shifts from 9.7/yr (no penalty) to 0.4-2.7/yr depending on λ; an HMM needs a k=20 rolling-mean smoothing window to reach ~2/yr. Strictly point-in-time protocol (fixed params, 3000-day-lookback online DP decode, 1-day execution delay, 5/10-day robustness): SPX 1990-2023 Sharpe 0.68 (JM) vs 0.54 (HMM) vs 0.48 (B&H), MaxDD -26.6% vs -28.9% vs -55.2%, turnover 44% vs 141% — flickering shows up as 3× turnover. Honest failure mode: ~15-day detection latency at COVID onset/offset (Shu, Yu & Mulvey 2024, arXiv 2402.05272, Tables 3-4, §3.4.2; published as J. Asset Mgmt 25(5), DOI 10.1057/s41260-024-00376-x; code github.com/Yizhan-Oliver-Shu/jump-models). Methodological origin is Bemporad, Breschi, Piga & Boyd 2018 (Automatica, "Fitting Jump Models"); the greedy *online* variant with RV features beats a correctly-specified HMM MLE under misspecification "in most settings" and was applied to SPX states (Nystrup, Kolm & Lindström 2020, JFDS 2(3):25-39, abstract-verified; accepted-manuscript PDF recovered via DTU Orbit). [peer-reviewed]
- **Regime-gated mixture-of-experts with observable-state routing works, with a catch.** Calm pool {GRU, HAR-RV, XGBoost} vs stress pool {GARCH-t, FIGARCH, HAR-RV}, logistic gates on VIX/curve/credit state variables: cross-asset median QLIKE 0.3232 vs HAR-RV 0.3603 (~10%) and vs rolling-best 0.3653; ~24% QLIKE cut in the high-vol regime vs rolling-best; DM significant at 5% on SPY/QQQ/IWM (arXiv 2604.10402, 2026, Table II Panels A-B, Table V). The catch: in the high-vol regime the router (0.3685) is *beaten by the ex-post best single specialist* (GARCH-t 0.2879, FIGARCH 0.2958) — routing captures only part of the theoretical specialization benefit. Target is a Garman-Klass proxy, not intraday RV. [preprint, single-author, unreplicated]
- **The only genuinely point-in-time regime→graph pipeline published underperforms a dumb retrain schedule in crises.** H-ETE-GNN: rolling 250-day Hurst exponent (10-day decision window, s=6) swaps the effective-transfer-entropy edge matrix and fine-tunes the GNN — clean causal design, 47 regime changes over 2003-2024. But in the 2008 and 2020 crashes it loses to alternatives (2008: Hurst-triggered RMSE 0.5287 vs TE-GNN 0.2653, Granger-GNN 0.3631; 2020: 0.2798 vs periodic-retrain 0.2356 — corrected Table 9/10 values; the originally harvested numbers were fabricated by an extraction pass and are superseded). No HAR or GARCH baseline anywhere in the paper; edges over Granger-GNN mostly not significant (MAPE p=0.146, corr p=0.354). 10 MSCI country ETFs, not SPX (Cho & Lee 2025, Fractal Fract 9(6):339, Tables 6-11, §2.3). [peer-reviewed, MDPI-tier, numbers corrected against live DOM]
- **"Regime-dependent GNN" in the 2026 title literature can mean nothing of the sort.** Kumar, Umeorah & Alochukwu 2026 (MDPI Mathematics 14(2):289, journal version of arXiv 2410.16858) implements only piecewise-static graphs estimated separately for train/val/test — no regime detector, no gating, no state-dependent adjacency. TemporalGAT beats HAR-RV by ~10% MSE (0.01165 vs 0.01298, Table 3), on a squared-daily-return proxy — and the published version *downgraded* from the arXiv precursor's true intraday RV. Errors are markedly higher in 2008-09 (MAPE 7.9-18.8% at h=1) than calm 2014-16. [peer-reviewed, MDPI-tier]
- **SPX-specific regime-HAR evidence exists but is thin.** Coefficient-based clustering (segment-wise OLS HAR coefficients → PCA → Bayesian GMM) feeding regime-specific HARs cuts MSE vs plain HAR by 8.52%/9.73%/11.9% (pre-COVID/COVID/post-COVID; paper's ×10^6-scaled MSE: 3.11 vs 3.40, 31.91 vs 35.35, 7.60 vs 8.63) on SPX 5-min RV 2014-2025 (Blake, Gandhi & Jakkula 2025, arXiv 2510.03236, Tables 2-4, §5). Caveats that cap its weight: MSE/MAPE only (no QLIKE, no DM/MCS — confirmed absent), margins over the Gaussian-HMM detector are noise-level in 2 of 3 periods (0.3%, 0.8%), horizons differ across period tables (5d vs 10d), "COVID" window is 2018-05 to 2020-09, and it's a Columbia undergraduate-program preprint. [preprint]
- **Cross-sectional jump spillovers only pay after stripping the common factor.** Idiosyncratic-network specifications (GHAR-JV / GNNHAR-JV) reduce one-day-ahead MSE by ~30% vs standard HAR on S&P 100 single names 2007-2021, while jump models on raw or market-based networks "deliver little benefit" (Cartea, Cucuringu & Fang 2026, SSRN 6333798; verbatim abstract recovered from OMI Newsletter March 2026, p.3 — full text still paywalled, point-in-time network estimation unverifiable, MSE only, cross-sectional single-name target not SPX index). Same newsletter, context-only: morning VVIX at 10:00 EST predicts next-day variance-asset returns (t up to 5.6, adj R² 2.6%, Sharpe > 2.0 after costs, predictability dead after 11:00 EST) — a candidate vol-of-vol node feature (Hizmeri & Bevilacqua, OMI Newsletter Mar 2026, p.3). [preprint, abstract-only]
- **Well-separated regimes ≠ big forecast gains.** Triple-timeframe MS-GARCH on EUR/USD: regime separation is statistically enormous (Calm-vs-Turbulent KS p=1.35e-153, Table 5) yet the MoE forecast edge is only -1.87% RMSE (0.036711 vs 0.037411, DM=+4.70, p=1.28e-6, Table 3) — and that headline RMSE is EWMA-smoothed output with no raw comparison, no QLIKE, and filtered-vs-smoothed routing input left ambiguous by the paper itself (Chaudhary 2026, arXiv 2606.06190). A clean calibration point for expectations. [preprint, EUR/USD, independent researcher]

## Contradictions & Open Threads

1. **Does a regime layer help point-vol forecasts at all?** Yes under QLIKE on CSI 300 HARQ (-5.1%, Fang & Ślepaczuk 2026, Table 2); yes at weekly/monthly but mixed daily on an 8-index panel incl. SPX (Ding et al. 2025, Tables 4-7); **no** for MS-GARCH vs a fitted single-regime GARCH (JRFM 2025, Table 6, ratios > 1) and for Fed-FEDS THAR under QLIKE (already in repo). The discriminating variables appear to be (a) the loss function, (b) whether the baseline is properly re-estimated, (c) horizon. **Resolving experiment on our data:** rolling HAR-WLS (JLDC spec, 630-day window) ± filtered two-state MS-GJR-GARCH residual probability as one extra regressor, SPX 5-min RV, h ∈ {1, 5, 22}, QLIKE + DM + MCS, regime-conditional breakdown. This is the cheapest high-information build in the whole program.
2. **Crisis-concentrated vs broad-based gains.** Fang & Ślepaczuk's own Table 4 contradicts the folk claim: the most significant DM stat (3.09, p=0.0010) is in the *low/normal-vol* bucket. Meanwhile the MoE router earns its 24% specifically in high-vol (arXiv 2604.10402, Table II) but loses to the ex-post best specialist there. The repo's open thread since 2026-05-12 ("does regime-conditional QLIKE reveal crisis-concentrated ensemble benefits?") now has direct but conflicting evidence — settle it with the regime-conditional QLIKE breakdown in experiment 1.
3. **Regime-conditional graphs vs a single dynamic graph.** No credible head-to-head exists. The one causal implementation (Cho & Lee 2025) loses to naive periodic re-estimation in both crises (Tables 9-11), and the one "regime-dependent" title paper has no regime mechanism (Kumar et al. 2026). **Experiment:** on the 34-symbol universe, compare (i) monthly re-estimated single graph (GNNHAR template), (ii) jump-model-state-triggered graph re-estimation, (iii) two per-regime graphs soft-blended by filtered probability — QLIKE + DM at h=1/5. Prior from this sweep: (i) wins or ties; regime-conditioning of the *graph* is the lowest-priority component.
4. **Abrupt vs smooth regime transitions.** Ding et al. 2025 prefers abrupt Markov switching over smooth-transition HAR; the jump-model family effectively imposes persistence-penalized abrupt switching and beats HMMs (Shu et al. 2024). Consistent direction, but neither tests the other's winner. Open: MS-TVTP-HAR vs jump-model-conditioned HAR on the same panel.
5. **Filtered-vs-smoothed ambiguity persists even in good papers.** Ding et al. 2025 never states which probabilities feed its recursive OOS forecasts; Chaudhary 2026 never disambiguates the routing tensor; Blake et al. 2025 leaves its 5-day RV smoothing (trailing vs centered) unspecified. Only Fang & Ślepaczuk 2026 and Shu et al. 2024 are explicitly clean. Any replication must pin the protocol: filtered probabilities, parameters re-estimated on training windows only, forecast-time features strictly lagged.
6. **Proxy quality confound.** Three of the regime wins (Kumar 2026, JRFM 2025, Cho & Lee 2025) are measured on squared-daily-return or 20-day-window proxies, not intraday RV. Whether regime layers add value against a *5-min RV target with a properly fitted HAR* is exactly what the literature does not settle — and what our data can.

## New vs Already Known

The repo already had the GNN half of this question swept same-day (GNNHAR ceiling, graph-construction contradiction, hybrid blending-beats-stacking verdict, DY-bridge, leakage taxonomy) and the HAR-fortress/evaluation canon from 2026-05-31; regime detection was the flagged near-zero gap. This brief fills it: 13 unique verified sources (15 dossier entries), 11 PDFs downloaded into `reference/project-papers/`, two paywalled journals recovered via institutional repositories. The material deltas are (1) a quantified, leakage-clean template for the highest-value first build — filtered MS-GARCH probability as one extra HAR/HARQ regressor, ~5% QLIKE on CSI 300, with the peer-reviewed caveat that daily-horizon gains are mixed and the counter-evidence that regime layers fail against fitted single-regime benchmarks under QLIKE; (2) the statistical jump-model family (Bemporad 2018 → Nystrup 2020 → Shu 2024) as the modern anti-flickering detector with code and an explicit point-in-time protocol, superseding vanilla hmmlearn HMMs; (3) the finding that the regime×GNN fusion layer — the question's Part III — has effectively no credible published incumbent: one title-only impostor and one causal implementation that loses to periodic retraining in crises. That makes the fusion layer simultaneously the most novel and least evidence-backed component; the evidence ranking for build order is regime-feature-into-HAR first, regime-gated ensembles second, regime-conditioned graphs last.

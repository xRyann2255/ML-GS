# Deep Research: What beats HAR for daily realized-volatility forecasting in 2024-2026?

**Date:** 2026-05-31 internship session · **Sources:** 11 kept of ~16 harvested (9 verified dossier + 2 fresh web-grounded)

## Direct Answer

For **daily, univariate, equity-index RV under QLIKE** — the exact cell our project lives in — essentially **nothing beats a properly-fitted rolling HAR with statistical significance.** Across the most rigorous 2024-2025 evidence (HARd-to-Beat on 1,445 stocks; Fed FEDS 2025 on S&P 500; Branco-Rubesam-Zevallos on 10 indices) the HAR family sits inside the Model Confidence Set far more often than any neural net, and where regime-switching or ML variants edge ahead on MSE, the advantage **collapses to insignificance under QLIKE specifically**. ML/modern methods win **reliably only when you change the problem**: (1) richer information sets — options-implied / rough-Heston spot vol (+5.8% QLIKE, DM p<0.01), LOB microstructure, cross-asset; (2) **multivariate realized covariance**, where graph methods genuinely beat HAR-DRD (GHAR +1.8% QLIKE, MCS p=1.000); (3) **longer horizons** (h=5, h=22) where ML's higher persistence approximates long memory. The single biggest caveat for us: **almost every "win" is on a different asset universe, sample, and often a different loss (MSE/Sharpe, not QLIKE)** than ours, and the most eye-catching deep-learning claims (transformers, GNNs-for-portfolios, foundation models) ship with weak or absent DM/MCS testing and unadjusted multiple-comparison risk. Confidence: **high** that univariate daily QLIKE is HAR's turf; **medium** that the covariance/options/long-horizon wins replicate on our data.

## Key Insights

- **The rolling-window fitting scheme matters more than the model class.** [peer-reviewed] HARd-to-Beat (IJF 2025): HAR-WLS QLIKE ≈ 0.313-0.314 vs a feedforward NN at 0.571 (≈82% worse); HAR sits in the MCS **85.5%** of the time vs FNN's **36.4%**, across 1,445 US stocks. Correctly specified HAR (the paper uses a **630-day ≈ 1.7-year** window with daily re-estimation — *not* the "2.5-4 years" mis-stated in our prior harvest) is the thing to beat.
- **On QLIKE specifically, even regime-switching HAR's edge evaporates.** [peer-reviewed] Fed FEDS 2025-061: THAR/STHAR beat HAR *and* all ML (XGBoost, FFN, LSTM, GRU) on **MSPE** — but the authors themselves state significant pairwise differences "largely disappear" under **QLIKE** (e.g. 2023 calm period: STHAR 0.038 vs HAR 0.051, DM non-significant). Our prior note overstated this as a clean QLIKE win; it is not.
- **Gradient boosting does NOT beat HAR at the daily horizon** on the cleanest read of the Intraday Commonality paper. [peer-reviewed] At h=1-day, OLS-Augmented (0.178 QLIKE) and HAR-D-Augmented (0.180) **beat** XGBoost-Augmented (0.187); XGBoost/LSTM only win at **intraday** horizons (10/30/65-min). The widely-circulated "XGBoost 0.1219 vs HAR 0.1482" figures **do not appear in the paper** and are a harvesting artifact — discard them.
- **Options-implied / rough-volatility augmentation is the most credible univariate win.** [preprint] HAR-RV-RHeston: QLIKE 0.0428 → 0.0403 (**+5.8%**), MAE −9%, DM stat −3.12 (p<0.01), robust across h=1-22. Real OOS, Newey-West errors. Caveat: single asset (S&P 500), 6-month COVID-spanning test window, and it needs an options surface + parametric calibration infra — not pure ML.
- **Graph methods win on multivariate realized *covariance*, not univariate RV.** [peer-reviewed] GHAR + Graphical-LASSO: QLIKE **−1.8%** vs HAR-DRD, Frobenius −2.5%, **MCS p=1.000**, 27 DJIA names, rolling 1,000-obs windows 2011-2021. This is a genuine, significance-backed win — but it answers the *covariance* question, adjacent to our daily-RV target.
- **Cross-market GNN-HAR wins scale with horizon — but only demonstrated on MSE/MAE, never QLIKE.** [peer-reviewed] DCRNN-HAR (J. Forecasting 2026): SPX MSE −13.2% (h=1) → −54.4% (h=22) across 8 global indices. Section 4.1 explicitly uses only MSE/MAE; **QLIKE is never computed.** Treat the horizon-scaling pattern as real, the QLIKE transfer as unproven.
- **GNNs for portfolios beat HAR on point error but the result is methodologically soft.** [preprint] sp500-gnn (arXiv 2026): best GNN MSE 0.0298 vs HAR 0.0329 (−9%), GNN-Sector Sharpe 0.984 vs HAR 0.635 — but **no DM, no MCS**, a 48-model hyperparameter sweep with no multiple-comparison correction, **weekly** (not daily) RV, MSE/Sharpe (not QLIKE), and a per-stock (not pooled) HAR baseline. Proof-of-concept, not evidence.
- **Time-series foundation models (TimesFM/Chronos/Moirai): the one RV-specific paper claims a win but the headline metric is ambiguous.** [preprint] arXiv 2505.11163, 21 global indices 2000-2021, runs DM + MCS. It reports the incrementally-trained TFM64IL^log beating the benchmark on QLIKE — **but the "skill score" direction is self-contradictory in the text** (the paper defines skill = loss-ratio, where >1 should mean *worse*, yet claims >1 = better), and the benchmark anchor is **ARFIMA, not HAR**. The win is *fine-tuned/incrementally-trained*, not zero-shot; pure zero-shot is only "a reasonable baseline." Do not bank this number until the direction is resolved from the PDF tables.
- **Transformers — one fresh 2025 peer-reviewed claim of a daily-QLIKE win, low-to-medium credibility.** [peer-reviewed, low-tier venue] MDPI JRFM 18(12):685 (2025): LSTM/CNN-LSTM/PatchTST-lite/Vanilla-Transformer vs HAR-RV/ARIMA/GARCH on SPX/NDX/DJI, 2000-2025, QLIKE at h=1/5/22 with DM tests; abstract says transformers achieve lowest QLIKE "particularly at short horizons." **No exact deltas in the abstract, MDPI venue, single research group** — flag as suggestive, not authoritative, and directly contradicts HARd-to-Beat's large-sample finding.

## Contradictions & Open Threads

- **HARd-to-Beat / Fed FEDS (HAR wins QLIKE) vs MDPI-JRFM 2025 + foundation-model paper (transformers/FMs win QLIKE).** The large-sample, high-rigor studies say HAR; the new deep-learning claims come from smaller samples, lower-tier venues, or ambiguous metrics. **Experiment to resolve on OUR data:** run HARd-to-Beat's HAR-WLS (630-day rolling, daily re-estimate) vs PatchTST and a fine-tuned TimesFM on our equity-index RV; pre-register a single QLIKE comparison at h=1/5/22, require **DM p<0.05 AND MCS inclusion**, and count every NN architecture/HP config as a trial against the deflated Sharpe / multiple-comparison budget. Prediction: HAR in the MCS at h=1; any ML win, if real, shows up at h=22.
- **MSPE-wins vs QLIKE-wins.** Fed FEDS (regime HAR), DCRNN-HAR, and sp500-gnn all report MSE/MSPE improvements that are *never demonstrated to survive under QLIKE*. **Criterion:** re-evaluate each method's forecasts under Patton-robust QLIKE; treat an MSE win that flips to QLIKE-insignificance as no win. This is the single most common way the literature overstates ML.
- **Skill-score direction in arXiv 2505.11163 is unresolved** — the text contradicts itself on whether >1 means better or worse, and the anchor may be ARFIMA not HAR. Resolve by reading the PDF tables directly; until then, do not cite a foundation-model QLIKE number.
- **Univariate vs covariance.** The cleanest significance-backed wins (GHAR, MCS p=1.000) are on *realized covariance*, not daily univariate RV. **Open question:** does the project want the univariate-RV problem (HAR's fortress) or the covariance problem (where graphs demonstrably help and the desk arguably cares more for portfolio risk)? The answer reshapes which SOTA is relevant.
- **Christensen-Siggaard-Veliyev (ML beats HAR, gains rise with horizon) vs Branco-Rubesam-Zevallos (no nonlinear ML beats HAR-X via MCS).** Both peer-reviewed, opposite conclusions. The reconciliation is the *information set*: CSV's gains are largest with firm+macro features; BRZ neutralizes the gain by giving HAR the same extended predictors (HAR-X). **Implication for us:** the win is in features, not architecture — give the linear baseline every feature the ML model gets before claiming an ML edge.

The repo already knew the headline (HAR is hard to beat univariate; wins come from features/horizon/covariance) and held most of these papers. This brief adds five things notes/ did not have crisply: (1) **two harvested numbers are now flagged false** — the "XGBoost 0.1219 vs HAR 0.1482 daily" figures are absent from the Intraday-Commonality paper (it actually shows HAR-D ≥ XGBoost at h=1), and HARd-to-Beat's window is **630 days (~1.7y), not 2.5-4y**; (2) the **Fed FEDS QLIKE caveat** — regime-HAR's much-cited superiority is an MSPE result that the authors say vanishes under QLIKE, our primary loss; (3) a **net-new strongest univariate win** (rough-Heston options augmentation, +5.8% QLIKE, DM p<0.01) that validates our options/VRP feature layer; (4) **JLDC/HARd-to-Beat as a ready-to-port Python HAR+ML harness** with the exact rolling-window spec and QLIKE losses — the fastest path to a defensible baseline on the GS machine; (5) the **2025-26 frontier (transformers, TS foundation models, LLM in-context learning)** is now mapped, with the honest verdict that every modern-architecture QLIKE claim so far is either MSE-only, significance-free, low-tier-venue, or metric-ambiguous — none yet clears the DM+MCS bar on daily equity-index RV.
